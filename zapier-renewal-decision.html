Your Zapier Renewal Is a Decision, Not a Subscription

The most expensive email in your SaaS stack is the renewal notice. Not because the number is large, but because most teams approve it without doing the one piece of arithmetic that matters: what you paid per successful run, and what you would pay elsewhere for the same runs.

Zapier bills per task. Almost every step in a Zap is a task. That meter is generous when you are starting out — five steps, a few hundred runs a month, the bill is background noise. It turns punitive exactly when you succeed: more runs and more steps per run both feed the same meter. Teams usually discover this the month before renewal, when finance asks why the automation line item tripled while headcount did not.

Before you click renew, pull three numbers. They take twenty minutes and they change the negotiation — with Zapier, or with whoever replaces it.

1. Your real cost per 1,000 successful runs

Take last month's invoice and divide it by the runs that completed and produced the intended business outcome — not total tasks, runs. Failed runs, retries and test executions consumed metered units too, so they belong on the cost side. As a purely illustrative example: a five-step workflow running 2,000 times a month is roughly 10,000 billable tasks on per-step metering. The number that matters is what those 2,000 outcomes cost you, because that is the unit any competing platform has to beat.

2. Your step count per run

Per-task metering punishes complexity, not just volume. A three-step Zap and a twelve-step Zap can serve the same business purpose and cost 4× different amounts. Audit your ten highest-volume Zaps and count steps. If your workflows grew by accretion — a filter here, a formatter there, an error handler someone added in March — you are paying a complexity tax that a flatter rebuild would erase on any platform.

3. The break-even volume against the alternatives

Migration has a one-time cost: rebuilding, re-testing, re-training. The meter is forever. So the question is never "is Make cheaper than Zapier" in the abstract — it is "at my run volume and my step count, how many months until the cheaper meter pays back the migration." That is arithmetic you can do on a napkin once you have numbers 1 and 2. Teams below the break-even line should renew and re-audit in a year; teams above it are donating money every month they wait.

Where switchers usually land

  • Make is the lowest-friction switch for Zapier teams: visual builder, similar concepts, and per-credit metering that tends to be kinder to multi-step workflows at small and mid-size volumes.
  • n8n is where high-volume teams land when the meter itself is the problem: per-execution metering, and the only serious self-hosting option among the three — at which point your bill becomes your server cost.

Neither verdict survives a change in your numbers. That is the point: renewals are arithmetic, not loyalty.
