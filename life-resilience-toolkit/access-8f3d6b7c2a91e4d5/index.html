2. 72-hour readiness planner

  • Water: plan roughly 3 L per person per day.
  • Keep food you already eat and rotate it.
  • Maintain essential medication and hygiene items.
  • Keep a flashlight and charged power bank ready.
  • Store a backup charging cable.
  • Keep some cash for outages.
  • Keep critical contacts accessible offline.

Estimated annual leak: $0

4. Home backup map

  • Payments: at least two usable methods.
  • Power: battery + cable + light.
  • Data: cloud or external backup.
  • Communication: offline emergency contacts.
  • Essentials: a second source for food, medicine and hygiene supplies.

5. Food-use system

  1. Create an “eat first” zone.
  2. Buy only what has a storage location.
  3. Freeze leftovers before they become waste.
  4. Keep a small buffer of repeat-use staples.
  5. Replace what you consume instead of building a forgotten stockpile.

6. 7-day implementation plan

  1. Give keys, wallet and chargers fixed homes.
  2. Audit fridge and pantry for items to use first.
  3. Add a small buffer of water, food and essentials.
  4. Cancel or downgrade one recurring money leak.
  5. Build three low-cost ways to rest or have fun.
  6. Verify device and file backups.
  7. Identify three single points of failure and create one backup route for each.

Monthly 15-minute reset

Check food → money leaks → misplaced items → readiness supplies → digital backups. The goal is not constant organization. The goal is a system that needs very little attention.
