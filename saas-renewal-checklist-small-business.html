Before renewal, answer these eight questions.

1. Who owns the tool?

Name the person accountable for usage, cost and the renewal decision. Unowned subscriptions tend to renew by inertia.

2. What workflow does it support?

Write the business workflow in one sentence. “We use it for AI” is not yet a value case.

3. Is it actually used?

Check active users, frequency and whether the workflow still matters. Low usage is a signal to investigate, not an automatic cancellation rule.

4. Is another tool doing the same job?

Compare capabilities across writing, meetings, automation, analytics, storage and AI assistants. Duplicate capability often hides across different vendor names.

5. Are seats, credits or tiers oversized?

Separate the question “Do we need the product?” from “Do we need this much of the product?”

6. Did AI change the economics?

Usage-based AI charges, premium add-ons and token consumption can turn a predictable subscription into variable spend. Recalculate the annual case.

7. What is the renewal window?

Record renewal date, cancellation notice period and switching effort early enough that the decision remains reversible.

8. What is the explicit decision?

Choose KEEP, REDUCE, CONSOLIDATE, REVIEW or CANCEL. “Renew by default” should not remain the hidden sixth option.

Use a simple decision ladder.

If the spend is small and obvious, keep the process lightweight. If the stack is large enough that ownership, overlap and renewal timing are hard to see, use more structure only when the value of the decision justifies it.

Why this problem is getting harder with AI spend.

Traditional SaaS renewals were often seat-based and predictable. AI introduces more variable consumption through tokens, API calls, model choice, credits and agent activity. That makes ownership, usage visibility and renewal discipline more important, not less.
