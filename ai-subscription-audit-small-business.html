The problem has changed.

For many small businesses, the question is no longer whether AI is useful. The harder question is whether the growing stack of AI assistants, automation platforms, embedded add-ons and SaaS subscriptions is producing enough measurable value to justify its cost and complexity.

Recent small-business research points to a consistent gap: adoption is increasing, but implementation knowledge, time, cost, security and ROI remain real barriers. That creates a practical opportunity for a simpler discipline: review the current stack before adding another tool.

Five signs you need an AI subscription audit

1 · UsageYou cannot say which paid AI tools were actually used in the last 30 days.If usage is unknown, renewal is happening on assumption rather than evidence.
2 · OverlapTwo or more paid products perform the same job.Writing, meeting notes, search, automation, analytics and support features increasingly overlap across vendors.
3 · OwnershipNobody clearly owns the business result.A tool can be technically useful and still be a bad purchase if no one owns its workflow, cost and renewal decision.
4 · ROIThe team can describe features but not the baseline they improved.Without a before-and-after measure, “AI value” becomes difficult to distinguish from enthusiasm.
5 · RenewalThe renewal date is more visible on the credit-card statement than in the operating process.Auto-renewal should not become the default decision mechanism.

A 15-minute first pass

  1. List every paid AI or SaaS product with a meaningful recurring cost.
  2. For each tool, write one sentence describing the workflow it supports.
  3. Record owner, users, monthly or annual cost and next renewal date.
  4. Mark obvious overlap and tools with little or no recent use.
  5. Choose one decision: KEEP / REDUCE / CONSOLIDATE / REVIEW / CANCEL.

This is enough to expose the obvious decisions. Do not start with a complex procurement program if a simple inventory already reveals the problem.
