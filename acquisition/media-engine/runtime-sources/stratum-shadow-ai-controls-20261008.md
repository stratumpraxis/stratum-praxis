## Buyer problem to test
An operations lead cannot answer which work processes are using personal or unapproved AI, what company information those workflows touch, or who reviews consequential outputs. Their immediate need is a small, defensible inventory and a prioritization decision, not a promise to make AI agents fully autonomous.

## Practical free diagnostic content
1. List three actual work tasks where AI has entered the process (not just the names of AI subscriptions).
2. For each task, identify the owner, data category, AI service, and how access is granted.
3. Separate low-risk drafting from workflows that touch confidential, regulated, personal, financial or customer information.
4. Mark where model output becomes a business action and where human verification is needed.
5. Check whether evidence of review, errors, exceptions and responsibility is retained.
6. End with one limited decision per workflow: continue with controls, pause, reduce access, or seek specialist advice. Do not infer regulatory compliance from this checklist.

## Safe editorial claims
- Employee AI usage can precede formal purchasing or security review, so teams benefit from checking actual workflows rather than relying only on an official tools list.
- The quality of an AI governance decision improves when the use case, data, owner, permissions, review step and exception handling are explicit.
- Tool access and data access are distinct from evidence that an output is suitable for a business decision.
