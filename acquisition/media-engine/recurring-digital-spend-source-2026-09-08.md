# Owner-approved source package: recurring digital spend deserves a keep/reduce/replace decision

This source package supports public derivative writing for Stratum Praxis / MarketNest Pro. It summarizes owner-supplied public market signals plus independently verifiable public evidence. It is not a customer case study and must not imply that Stratum Praxis produced any of the reported savings.

Core thesis:
A recurring digital bill is not just a price tag. The useful decision is whether the software, cloud resource, AI tool, or infrastructure line still earns its place after usage, overlap, switching cost, architecture, renewal timing, and currency/payment friction are considered. The practical output is a KEEP / REDUCE / CONSOLIDATE / REPLACE / STOP decision, not blanket cost cutting.

- In an owner-supplied X post dated 2026-09-05, TaraT (@tarat_211) said a side project had been paying Firebase $40/month and was moved to an $8 VPS the author already had, making the claimed marginal cost $0. Treat this as the author's anecdote, not an independently audited saving.

Allowed concepts:
- Treat recurring software and cloud spend as a portfolio of decisions, not a pile of invoices.
- A cheaper replacement is valuable only if it preserves the outcome that matters and does not create larger migration, reliability, security, or operator costs.
- Before adding another subscription, inspect duplicate capability, weak usage, oversized plans, upcoming renewals, and infrastructure that no longer matches the workload.
- For globally billed services, normalize the decision to the currency actually paid and include payment/FX friction where relevant.
- Use KEEP / REDUCE / CONSOLIDATE / REVIEW / REPLACE / STOP as decision states rather than assuming every low-usage line should be cancelled.
- Small public anecdotes can reveal buyer language and recurring pain; they are not proof of a universal savings rate.
- The free Stratum Praxis AI & SaaS Waste Calculator can be used to size the portion of recurring software spend worth reviewing. It produces scenarios from the user's own inputs and does not promise savings.
- The existing $499 AI & SaaS Spend Waste Audit is appropriate only when the decision value is large enough to justify a deeper review.

Public derivative goal:
Create a useful article for founders, small teams, creators, and digital operators who pay recurring software/cloud bills, especially when some costs are billed internationally. Start from the idea that the interesting number is not the subscription price but the cost of the outcome. Show a simple decision sequence:
1. annualize the recurring spend;
2. identify the workflow/outcome each line protects;
3. isolate overlap, idle capacity, renewal inertia, or avoidable infrastructure;
4. include switching and currency/payment friction;
5. choose KEEP / REDUCE / CONSOLIDATE / REVIEW / REPLACE / STOP;
6. re-measure after the change.
