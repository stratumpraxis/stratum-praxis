Example company: a small bookkeeping team handling recurring monthly client-document collection and close preparation. All names, volumes and financial assumptions below are illustrative.

The best first automation is not a general chatbot. It is the narrow loop around identifying missing client documents, drafting reminders, scheduling follow-ups and synchronizing status for human review.

2. Current workflow map

  1. Team member reviews the client checklist before close.
  2. Missing statements, receipts or answers are identified manually.
  3. A reminder is drafted and sent to the client.
  4. The team checks for a response and repeats the follow-up when needed.
  5. Status is copied into the internal close tracker.
  6. Exceptions and sensitive items are escalated to a human owner.
Primary leak: repetitive detection, drafting and status-sync consume attention even though most cases follow a predictable pattern. The exception path still requires human judgment.

4. ROI scenario

Planning assumptions only. Replace these with the buyer's real baseline before making an investment decision.

Monthly workload80 client files × 3 follow-up touches × 6 minutes = 24 hours/month.
Loaded labor assumption$42/hour → approximately $1,008/month of current handling capacity.
Pilot targetReduce repetitive handling time by 50% while keeping exceptions human-reviewed → 12 hours/month recovered.
Gross capacity value12 hours × $42 = $504/month before tool and setup costs.

Illustrative tool/integration cost: $80/month. Illustrative setup effort: 6 hours × $42 = $252. Under these assumptions, first-year net capacity value would be approximately $4,836 before tax and before any additional implementation/support costs.

Capacity value is not guaranteed cash savings or revenue. It only becomes economically useful if the recovered time is actually redeployed, avoided, or converted into higher-value work.

5. Risk + human checkpoints

  • Do not send reminders when the client has already supplied the document through an untracked channel.
  • Do not expose one client's information to another client or an unauthorized system.
  • Keep tax, accounting, legal and judgment-heavy responses human-led.
  • Log every automated action so the team can audit failures and stop the workflow quickly.
  • Start with redacted or synthetic test data before any production rollout.

6. 30-day pilot plan

Days 1–5 · BaselineMeasure volume, handling time, exception types and current response latency.
Days 6–12 · Shadow modeGenerate suggested actions without sending them. Compare with human decisions.
Days 13–21 · Limited live pilotAutomate only low-risk reminders for a bounded client subset with approval checkpoints.
Days 22–30 · DecisionCompare time saved, error rate, client response and operating cost. Scale, redesign or stop.

7. Pilot success thresholds

  • At least 40% reduction in repetitive handling time.
  • No material privacy or client-data incidents.
  • No increase in incorrect or duplicate reminders.
  • Human exception handling remains clear and fast.
  • Projected annual capacity value remains comfortably above operating cost.
