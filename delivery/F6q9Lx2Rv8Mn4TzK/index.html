Overdue Invoice Recovery Kit

A practical follow-up system for freelancers and small service businesses. Use it to move from polite reminder → firm follow-up → final internal escalation without rewriting every message from scratch.

Operational communication templates only. Not legal, debt-collection, tax or accounting advice. Do not threaten remedies you are not entitled to use; check your contract and local law before applying fees or taking formal action.

1. Recovery sequence

  1. Before due date: confirm invoice receipt and payment date.
  2. 1–3 days overdue: short, neutral reminder; attach/relink invoice.
  3. 7 days overdue: request a specific payment date and identify any invoice dispute.
  4. 14 days overdue: firm follow-up, restate amount, due date, and requested resolution date.
  5. 21+ days overdue: pause new discretionary work where contractually appropriate and decide whether to escalate internally, renegotiate, or seek professional advice.

7. Decision rules

  • Do not keep sending the same message. Every follow-up should ask for either a date, a dispute reason, or a resolution.
  • Separate collection from emotion. Keep a written timeline and invoice facts.
  • Do not invent late fees. Only reference fees or interest when valid under your agreement and applicable law.
  • Stop scope creep. Do not add new unpaid work while an old invoice remains unresolved unless you deliberately choose to.
  • Escalate when economics justify it. For material balances, consider an accountant, lawyer, licensed collection professional, or other appropriate local professional.

8. 10-minute routine

  1. Sort overdue invoices by amount × days overdue.
  2. Send the next appropriate message, not a generic duplicate.
  3. Record the contact date and promised payment date.
  4. Schedule one next action.
  5. Close the loop when paid or formally disputed.
